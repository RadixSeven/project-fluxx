## Why test our formulas for the distributions
The ShiftedLogNormalDistribution parameters I see in the values fit to the data extracted in FHIR-3323-fy26-incremental-sync.fluxx don't seem right. There are two observations that make me question them:
- The 95th percentile is huge - frequently greater than 10,000 or 100,000. It seems unlikely that correct fitting of data whose highest value is 118.1667 would produce such an estimate for the 95th percentile
- The difference between the minimum and the mode is tiny. First, in log space, the distribution ought to be symmetric around the mode. Even if the 95th percentile is the square root of the actual, it's not close to making things symmetric.

## Testing our formula
So, please choose some known parameters for the mean and variance of the underlying distribution (u_mean and u_variance). We'll ignore the minimum for now - set it to 0. The mode should be exp(u_mean - u_variance). Sample from the distribution many times (maybe 1,000,000 samples). Calculate the 95th percentile of the samples and the median of the samples and use a KDE to approximate the mode. The median should be equal to exp(u_mean). Compare the theoretical median with the actual median. That should give you an estimate of the error due to sampling. Then compare the calcuated 95th percentile with the real one and the calculated mode with the real one. Do this for several pairs of values. For example, (ln(1), ln(2)), (ln(10), ln(4)) (ln(8), ln(16)). Use these calculated values to test the formula we have and to write approximate tests. Do the sampling and calculation 30 times for each pair. Then for each parameter calculated from the sample take the maximum and minimum values and quintuple their distance to the center (e.g, c=(max+min)/2 upper_bound=c+(max-c)*5, lower_bound=c-(max-c)*5). The expected value for any parameter generated by our formula should be between those upper and lower bounds. If that does not hold, there is an error in our formula.
